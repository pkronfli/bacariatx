# Bacari design model: changes by version

Newest first. Each version is listed on the page under Versions. Versions 1-18 were written as single files (design/versions/vN.js); from version 19 a version is a list of part files (design/versions/vN.json).

## Version 28 (Nov 25, 2026, Patrick)
The back (south) part of the upper deck, with its pergola, tables, planters and lights, is removed; the big glass door upstairs gets a steel Juliet railing and the east part of the deck stays.

## Version 27 (Nov 25, 2026, Patrick)
The bamboo along the east fence fills in to a solid green wall, about 17-21 ft tall, from the corner garden to the alley.

## Version 26 (Nov 25, 2026, Patrick)
The existing raised bed along the east fence is in, about 4 ft wide behind a low block wall and planted with bamboo; the east deck now stops at it.

## Version 25 (Nov 25, 2026, Patrick)
The high deck along W 6th is now 18 ft deep, the same as the east deck, and the bar is back in the middle of the courtyard with four seating pockets around it, split by river-rock cross paths and edged with planting, as in Robert's walkthrough.

## Version 24 (Nov 25, 2026, Patrick)
The statue is now Michelangelo's David, from a real 3D scan, about 7 ft tall on the plinth at the back fence, facing the bar.

## Version 23 (Nov 25, 2026, Patrick)
The back of the courtyard is now the same wooden fence as the rest of the property line, the statue is sculpted as a life-size man, and the teal walls and wooden slat screens are gone.

## Version 22 (Nov 25, 2026, Patrick, Robert)
The courtyard now ends at a tall wall in line with the back of the building, with a parking lot behind it; the fountain-garden changes from version 21 are undone, and a classical marble statue stands against the back wall at the end of the path from the bar.

## Version 21 (Nov 24, 2026, Patrick)
No deck past the back of the building: the low deck is gone and the courtyard is now a formal garden, with the bar as its fountain, four big curved planting beds between cross paths, and seating around them; the teal walls and wooden screens are removed.

Patrick's S015 and S017. The low deck along the back is removed and the medium deck on the east side now stops at the line of the back of the building (z 85), with a railing across its end and its stairs moved onto the bar's cross path. The garden runs at patio level to the tall back wall; the back strip is paved for two rows of tables, the long banquette and its own string-light canopy. The bar moves 10 ft toward the back to the middle of the bigger garden. River-rock paths form a cross through the bar (north to the high deck stairs, east to the medium deck stairs, south to the back seating, west to the restroom and service doors), and four big curved beds with corten edges about 1.5 ft tall fill the quarters, each with an olive or mountain laurel, lavender, sage, boxwood, grasses and flowers. The teal block walls, the turf lawn beside the building and the cedar slat screens are taken out. Tables sit around the beds: under the pergola, the north-east and south-east corners, the south-west corner and the rows along the back.

## Version 20 (Nov 24, 2026, Robert)

## Version 19 (Nov 24, 2026, Patrick)
The three boarded doors on the back wall of the building, onto the upper deck, are now glass doors.

## Version 18 (Nov 24, 2026, Patrick)
More Bacari-style night light: candles on every table (night only), fairy lights through all the oaks, a fire pit on the front terrace, Edison pendants over the bar and iron lanterns along the walls.

More Bacari-style night light, after the LA patios (fairy lights hanging from every branch of the big trees, candlelit tables, fire pits). Candles now show only at night: an amber glass hurricane with a flame and a soft halo on every table, and tea lights around the bar top. Every live oak (front, heritage, high deck, east, and the five leaning oaks in the corner garden) is wrapped in warm fairy lights along its limbs and through the underside of its canopy. A stone fire pit on the front terrace lawn, Edison-bulb pendants on a steel ring over the bar with a warm glow strip under the counter, iron lanterns along the tall back wall and the east fence, and warm fill light over the bar and the fire pit. All of it switches on with the Night button; by day the candles are put away.

## Version 17 (Nov 24, 2026, Patrick)
Streets corrected from map data and aerial imagery: W 6th one-way westbound with three lanes and north-curb parking, Pressler one-way north below W 6th, real curb cuts and driveways, and the north side of W 6th filled in.

Streets corrected from OpenStreetMap and Esri aerial imagery. W 6th is one-way westbound: three travel lanes with dashed white lines, a yellow edge line on the south (left) side, a parking lane along the north curb, westbound arrows and ONE WAY signs, and every car heading west; no crosswalks across W 6th at Pressler. Pressler is one-way northbound south of W 6th (arrows, no center line) and continues north of W 6th as a two-way street, with crosswalks across it on both sides of W 6th. Curb cuts where the imagery shows them: the neighbor's parking-lot driveway just east of the lot (x 138..152) on the south side; on the north side the parking-lot driveway (x 105..125), Winflo Dr (x 172..198) and a second lot entrance (x 246..262). The north side of W 6th now has ground at street level with its two parking lots and grass.

## Version 16 (Nov 24, 2026, Patrick)

The streets look like streets. W 6th is dark asphalt about 40 ft wide (z -52..-12) with a parking lane on each side, double yellow center line, white parking-lane lines, a 6 in concrete curb and gutter, sidewalks with 5 ft scored joints on both sides, ladder crosswalks at Pressler St, cobra-head street lights that glow at night, and parked and passing cars. Pressler St gets the same asphalt, a double yellow line down its slope, a west sidewalk and a crosswalk at the corner; the alley gets darker, patched asphalt.

## Version 15 (Nov 24, 2026, Patrick's suggestion S013)
Up-lights for the trees at night: in-grade well lights washing each big live oak and a small spike light in every planter tree, on top of version 14's night lighting.

Up-lights for the trees at night. Each of the four big live oaks (front terrace, heritage oak behind the building, high deck, east side) gets flush in-grade well lights around its trunk (in the deck boards on the decks) with a warm spotlight washing up the trunk into the canopy, and every corten planter tree gets a small brass spike up-light tucked in the open corner of the box. At night (Night button) the oak trunks, limbs and canopies and the olive and mountain laurel canopies pick up a warm glow. The five leaning trees in the corner garden keep their up-lights.

## Version 14 (Nov 24, 2026, Patrick)
Bacari-style night lighting everywhere (bistro string-light canopies over every deck, rattan pendants, table candles, wall sconces, path lights), a 20 ft automated sliding gate on the back lot, and the apartments behind can be hidden.

Bacari-style night lighting across the property: zigzag canopies of warm bistro string lights on black poles over the front terrace, the high, medium and low decks and the upper-deck pergola; rattan globe pendants under the pergola pocket; a glass votive candle on every table; brass wall sconces on the building; low path lights along the river-rock paths; and warm fill lights over each deck (all switch on with the Night button). The back service gate on the alley becomes a 20 ft automated sliding gate (steel frame with cedar slats, ground track, guide rollers, operator motor on a pad, photo-eye safety posts and a keypad pedestal on the alley side), drawn partly open. The apartments and the parking garage behind the lot are built in their own group (returned as backB) so the page can hide them.

## Version 13 (Nov 24, 2026, Patrick)
Every tree on the property is now drawn as a Texas live oak (short thick trunks, long twisting limbs, broad dark-green canopies), on top of version 12's light blue building.

Every tree on the property is drawn as what it is, a Texas live oak: a short, thick dark-barked trunk with a flared base, forking low into long, sinuous limbs that reach out more than up (a few big ones sweep out almost level), carrying a broad, dense, dark-green dome canopy much wider than it is tall, built from many leaf clusters in two greens. This covers the front oak, the heritage oak behind the building, the oak on the high deck, the east-side oak, and the five leaning trees in the corner garden (still leaning and up-lit at night). Canopies keep the traced footprints and clear the deck heads.
